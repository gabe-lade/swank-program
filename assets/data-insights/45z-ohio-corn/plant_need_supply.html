How much corn is no-tilled
If cover crops were added to more no-till corn

The Ag Census counts no-till across all crops, and corn is no-tilled less than beans. It also counts no-till and cover crops separately and does not say how many fields have both; taking the two as unrelated gives about 1 in 12 no-till corn acres with a cover crop today. The 1 in 4 and 1 in 2 options add cover crops beyond what the Census counts (roughly 1.6 and 2.5 times today's cover-crop acres), so they are what-if scenarios, not a different reading of the Census. Where a cover crop sits barely changes the total, because it cuts about as much carbon on a no-till field as on a tilled one. EQIP and H2Ohio cost-share can help pay for one.

At the same practice, each 0.1 lb less nitrogen per bushel harvested is worth about 10 cents more per bushel to a plant. Counties in the same USDA soil region score alike, so plant areas differ little; your own nitrogen rate matters more. Indiana counties are not scored, so for Greenville and the Indiana plants the averages cover the Ohio part of the circle. For your own fields, use the Ohio Corn & Wheat 45Z calculator; its figures come out about 10 cents lower because it measures against your own conventional field rather than USDA's default for undocumented corn.
